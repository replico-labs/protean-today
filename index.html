Overview

Protean is the governance layer for social messengers: programmable, on-chain governance mechanisms brought directly into Telegram, Discord, and Slack.

Instead of forcing every community into the same governance model, Protean provides modular governance primitives that deploy directly inside the chat a community already uses. A DAO's treasury and its voting logic are deliberately separate, so a group can change how it decides things without ever migrating funds.

The problem it solves

Communities are usually stuck choosing between two broken options. Web2 tools make collective ownership difficult, with no safe way to pool or spend group money inside a chat app. Web3 frameworks force people out of the chat onto a separate site, with a treasury permanently welded to one voting contract, so changing your mind about governance means a risky, expensive fund migration.

How Protean approaches it

Governance runs directly inside the chat a community already uses, with no separate site and no new habit to build. Because the treasury and the voting logic are kept apart, a community can change how it governs as it evolves, for example moving from a simple multisig to quadratic voting later, without ever touching the funds.

A passed proposal can already execute real onchain actions atomically through Protean's verified action library, reaching Treasury, tokens, and connected wrappers in one approved step. The planned offchain counterpart is a compliant banking tool that executes a payment the moment a DAO approves it, with no separate manual step required. Optional legal wrappers, such as a Wyoming DUNA or a Marshall Islands DAO LLC, are also available for communities that need real-world legal standing for their onchain treasury.

Architecture

Three parts: the governance contracts, the bot interfaces, and this site.

contractsSolidity: one factory per governance model, deployed with Foundry
botNode.js: handles all interaction and transaction execution on Telegram, Discord, and Slack
connectThis site: onboarding for people integrating the bot

Every DAO gets its own, fully separate contract deployment: a real Governance contract, a real Treasury, a real governance token. Nothing is a shared proxy; each community's DAO is independent from every other.

Two optional wrapper contracts sit alongside a DAO: an NFT marketplace wrapper for on-chain trading, and a Guard Wrapper, a security-council layer that can inspect and confirm sensitive treasury or token actions before they execute.

Governance models

All ten share the same propose → vote → queue → execute shape.

Token-weighted

The baseline model. Voting power is read from a snapshot at proposal creation, weighted by balance.

Quadratic

Same shape as token-weighted, but voting power scales with the square root of tokens held, reducing the influence of the largest holders relative to the wider group.

Liquid

Token-weighted voting with delegation. A holder can hand their voting power to someone else, and reclaim it at any time.

Optimistic

A proposal passes automatically once its challenge window closes, unless someone challenges it, in which case it goes to a real vote. Built for routine actions that shouldn't need a full vote cycle every time.

Conviction

No discrete "for/against" vote. A holder commits their current stake to a proposal, and their conviction accumulates the longer it stays committed, rewarding sustained, patient support over a single burst of votes.

Board

A signer multisig. No governance token at all: a fixed set of signers confirms transactions directly, with a configurable approval threshold.

Sortition

A council selected at random from eligible members via an external randomness source, serving fixed terms, governance by lottery rather than election or stake.

Delegate

A council elected by ordinary token-holder vote, serving fixed terms before a new election reopens.

Sowellian

A two-phase model: proposals first go through an approval phase, and disputed outcomes move to an adjudication phase, with optional resolution against a live Chainlink or Switchboard price feed.

Decision Markets

Futarchy. Two prediction markets are opened for a proposal, one betting it will pass and one betting it will fail, and the outcome is decided by comparing their time-weighted average price. No voting step exists in this model at all.

Opportunity Market

A confidential prediction market, a distinct primitive, not a twin of the ten governance models above.

Opportunity Market has no proposals and no votes. Its lifecycle is: list an opportunity, place fully encrypted bets on which one will succeed, resolve the outcome, then reveal only the winning opportunity and its aggregate backing total. Individual bets and positions are never revealed, even after resolution. Built on Zama's confidential compute stack, currently deployed on Ethereum Sepolia, since that's where Zama's coprocessor network exists today. It moves to other chains as Zama's network expands to them.

Module lifecycles

The actual states and commands each of the eleven modules moves through, from proposal to finished outcome.

Token-weighted, Quadratic, Liquid

Pending→ Active→ Succeeded / Defeated→ Queued→ Executed
Commands
/propose→ /vote→ /queue→ /execute

All three share this same eight-state shape (also including Cancelled and Expired). The only real difference between them is how voting power is counted: Token-weighted uses a raw balance snapshot, Quadratic uses the square root of that balance, and Liquid adds delegate() / undelegate(), letting a holder hand their vote to someone else and have it resolve down a full delegation chain.

Optimistic

ChallengeWindow→ if challenged→ Active→ Succeeded / Defeated→ Queued→ Executed
Commands
/propose→ /challenge (optional)→ /vote→ /queue→ /execute

The one model that starts in a different state than every other: ChallengeWindow, not Pending. A proposal passes automatically once that window closes unless someone calls challenge(), which is the only point a real token-weighted vote (Active) ever happens here.

Conviction

Pending→ support() accumulates→ Succeeded→ Queued→ Executed
Commands
/propose→ /support→ /withdrawsupport (optional)→ /queue→ /execute

No discrete vote at all, vote() isn't used here. A holder commits their current stake with support(), and conviction accumulates the longer that stake stays committed. withdrawSupport() pulls it back at any time.

Board

Proposed→ confirm() by signers→ auto-queued at threshold→ Executed
Commands
/propose→ /confirm→ /revoke (optional)→ /execute

The only model with no token and no separate queue step at all, confirming a proposal automatically queues it the moment the required threshold is reached. It's also the only model that can move backward: revoking a confirmation below threshold un-queues a proposal that had already qualified.

Sortition

registerEligible()→ startSortition()→ finalizeSortition()→ Council seated→ propose → vote → queue → execute
Commands
/registereligible→ /startsortition→ /finalizesortition→ /propose→ /vote→ /queue→ /execute

Council selection has no election at all. Eligible token holders opt into a pool, and the council is drawn from that pool by a verifiable random shuffle against an external randomness source. Once seated, anyone meeting the eligibility threshold (not just council members) can propose; only the council actually votes, by headcount, not token weight.

Delegate

startElection()→ declareCandidacy()→ voteInElection()→ Council seated→ council proposes & votes
Commands
/startelection→ /declarecandidacy→ /voteinelection→ /finalizeelection→ /propose→ /vote→ /queue→ /execute

Three separate mechanisms on one contract: an election open to every token holder (token-weighted), council proposals restricted to sitting council members only (headcount-weighted), and a separate recall vote, also open to every holder, that can remove a council member early before their term ends.

Sowellian

ApprovalVoting→ PositionsOpen→ Executed→ ResolutionPending→ if challenged→ Adjudicating→ Finalized
Commands
/proposecriteria→ /castapprovalvote→ /finalizeapproval→ /takeposition→ /execute→ /resolveviaoracle or /proposeresolution→ /challengeresolution → /castadjudicationvote → /finalizeadjudication, or /finalizeunchallenged→ /claimposition

A proposal first passes a token-weighted approval vote, then opens for people to takePosition() on its real-world outcome. Resolution can come automatically from a live oracle, or from a human proposing an outcome that others can challenge into a second, adjudication vote. claimPosition() only pays out once the outcome is final.

Decision Markets (Futarchy)

proposeWithSeed()→ two live markets open→ trade()→ finalizeProposal()→ Executed
Commands
/proposemarket→ /trade→ /finalizeproposal→ /execute

No vote of any kind exists on this contract. Proposing seeds two live trading markets, pass and fail, and people trade whichever side they believe in. finalizeProposal() simply compares the two markets' time-weighted average price. Pass must exceed fail's by a configured margin, that comparison alone is the entire verdict.

Opportunity Market

listOpportunity()→ deposit()→ back()→ resolve()→ reveal winning total→ computeReward()→ withdraw
Commands
/createmarket→ /listopportunity→ /deposit→ /back→ /resolve→ /revealwinningtotal→ /computereward→ /withdraw

No proposals, no votes, not a governance model at all. A deployer lists one or more opportunities; bettors fund a confidential balance and place fully encrypted bets with back(). Once the deployer calls resolve(), only the winning opportunity's aggregate total is ever revealed, individual bets and positions never are, even after everyone has been paid out.

Guard Wrapper

An optional security layer a DAO can adopt without changing its governance model at all.

A Guard Wrapper sits between governance and a DAO's treasury or token contract. Governance can propose an instruction to it, but a small set of signers must confirm before it actually executes, giving a DAO a human circuit-breaker against a malicious or mistaken proposal, without giving those signers any power to act on their own. Signers can only confirm or reject what governance already sent; they can never initiate a transaction themselves.
